Imagining being in a new job:

I'm so deeply afraid that I'll fail like I did at nvidia that it's prevented me from taking the necessary actions to search for another job. Somehow I got over that and am in a new job, though honestly if you asked me I wouldn't be able to tell you how.

I don't want to come off as pushy or needy, but I feel like I need a lot of reassurance to put my thoughts at ease. I have trouble concentrating because I'm constantly thinking about my failures at nvidia. I'm super paranoid about trying to read people's subtle non-verbal queues to suggest if they think I'm doing a good job or not.

I almost always feel like I'm not doing a good job so I feel the need to make up for it with work ethic. I need the reassurance from others to help convince myself that I'm not a terrible teammate.

If I can just work all day then I'll get more done and hopefully be able to meet the goals of the project (no bugs, on time, met scope). When I run into hard-to-find bugs I don't ask for help. Instead I analyze the heck out of the problem. If I need to learn a new technique, buy new tools, or scan through material online I do anything to solve the problem on my own. Being unable to do so means that I failed. Additionally, asking for help is making that failure public. I already know that I'm a failure, but if the people around me reassure me that they like working with me then I can still manage to push through and find motivation. But, asking for help/failing publicly might make people around me feel like I'm not competent which would jeopardize that source of reassurance.

Whenever I find a bug I enter a state of unrest. I feel like I can't do anything (outside of basic functions to maintain life) except try to resolve the bug as fast as possible. I want to know how it happened in the first place so I can avoid making the same mistake in the future. When I enter this state I ignore everyone outside of work: friends, family, romantic partners. I've skipped out on several vacations and holidays with last-minute notice because of this. I always regret doing so in hindsight, but I still proceed to do it everytime, convincing myself that resolving this bug ASAP is of the utmost importance. I'll basically live at the office if I believe that I can get it resolved faster that way.

I have an obsession with only delivering good news. I want to reassure people that I've got things under control. They can rest at ease with me on the case. So if I find a bug I generally provide an optimistic time to resolve. This instills confidence in the state of the project. In order to make up for the optimistic timeline I just work my ass off to get it fixed in the promised time.

I worry about not connecting with people. Basically, I need to care about the people I work with to help me justify this obsessive behavior. If I don't feel a strong connection to them then why am I sacrificing all other aspects of my life for this? While it sounds good on the surface to not have to obsess over work, not feeling a connection with coworkers makes it harder for me to get work done. Whenever I have any semblance of an abrasive or cold interaction with a coworker I'm unable to focus for hours/days/weeks thinking about the consequences of that interaction. What if they told their/my manager that I'm not good to work with? Will I get fired or put off the project? Then what about all of the unresolved work that I put into the project? I won't be around to complete it. I won't be able to make sure that the bugs were fixed. What if I leave the company, someone comes in after to fill my role and finds a bug that I wrote? Will they badmouth me to my former coworkers, saying I'm a terrible developer? Does that mean these coworkers will no longer have a good opinion of me? Then if they leave to work other places will they want to work with me again? Would they still recommend me to other companies? If my name becomes a dirty word at a previous company I can probably chalk up everything I worked for there as a waste. The only way to ensure that this isn't the case is to make sure I stay on the project until the end and make sure that I brought the project to a neat conclusion.

However, if I have a good relationship with my coworkers then I know that even if hard times come up I feel confident that they'll have my back.

Now that I'm working again I feel a renewed sense of purpose, but I start analyzing the ways in which the product I'm working on can help or hurt humanity. I want to make a lasting positive impact so I need the good to vastly outweigh the bad. Are people going to get hurt using this product (physically, mentally, emotionally)? Will this product prevent people from living in a way that they value? Am I just providing a way for people to escape reality? Am I just working to make the company money or do people genuinely need this project. I feel a strong desire to know this information. If I can't get these answers from the company then I fabricate a best guess. However, then I start to doubt that best guess. What if I'm wrong? What if this is actually going to cause harm to a lot of people? E.g. cell phones, have done a lot to connnect people, but they've also provided an escape for many which has overall make the world feel less personal.

Whenever I feel like my work might be causing too much harm, or not enough good, I develop an incredibly strong resistance to working. I find it hard to be motivated to do anything. I am paralyzed until I am reassured or convinced otherwise.

I frequently think back to experiences at Stryker and Nvidia.
From Stryker I have to avoid another situation like Shayad. That was so traumatic that I wasn't able to get any work done at the time. I started avoiding meetings and any interaction with him that I could. It was a giant mess and caused many HR-worthy altercations. My stress/anxiety still elevates when I think about it.
From Nvidia I need to establish a connection with coworkers early/often. I need to setup 1on1s with everyone I will interact with. Maybe this will help get the ball rolling such that am able to actually focus on work, not what other people think of me. If people are resistant to that then I fear I'll spiral in the same way as I did at Nvidia. I won't be able to concentrate, I'll just be thinking about all of the thoughts they could be having about me. If someone consistently or outright rejects 1on1 then I fear they think I'm slacking off and wasting their time. I expect they'll complain about me to their/my manager about how I'm just here wasting everyone's time.

I have a difficult time showing vulnerability. Whenever I say something like "I have a bug, I'm working on it" or "I wasn't able to meet the deadline, but I should be able to given X more days/weeks" then I fear that people will lose their confidence in my abilities unless I have a good relationship with them and can convince myself otherwise (they won't lose confidence in me). Once I fear that people have lost or are losing their confidence in me then I don't have anything to prop me up. My inner critic runs wild. I already think that I'm a failure, waste of talent, waste of resources. Now that others think that too how can I move on from that? I can't focus. I'm exhausted but I can't sleep. I am now more paranoid than ever that people think I'm doing a bad job that I'm unable to admit to failures. I now try to hide bugs or where exactly I'm stuck on a new feature. When people ask for status updates I don't respond. I just get in my own head about how terribly they must think of me for not having already completed the task.

Now that I'm hiding bugs I start to be paranoid about people reaching out to me asking for status updates. I don't want to lie, but I don't feel comfortable being vulnerable with them. I start to mark myself "offline" while I'm working or "call-in-sick" as an excuse to go offline, but in reality I'm still working on the problem, I just want to put my mind at ease that I won't receive communication that derails my ability to focus.

I've started to work off-hours again. I'm not "online" during normal working hours, but I am "online" when I don't expect other people to try to reach out to me. This way I at least have X hours to try to focus on the task. Then maybe I can get somewhere and be able to notify people that I've completed the task by their next work morning.

People are annoyed that I'm not available during normal working hours. How can I tell them that this is the only way I can focus on the task? They won't understand. It is stupid and cowardly so why would they accept that? They want to help me and make me feel included but I worry that it might be too late. Now that everyone has a bad opinion of me how do I claw my way out of that hole? The only way I can think of is to prove my competency. If I work hard and can get the job done then they'll slowly regain trust in me. Then maybe, over time, our relationship will sweeten such that I feel comfortable asking for help again.

However, this isn't how it goes. No. Instead I run into some problem that I'm not able to resolve on my own. My stupid pride and insecurity won't allow me to ask for help, but I can't solve it.

I try everything. I've been late on not only the original deadline, but also my extended deadline. Now I'm desparate. I'm only sleeping 3 hours a night. I'm barely eating and restricting water intake so that I don't spend too much time in the bathroom during the day. I don't look at my phone for days. That's a distraction. I buy books. I try to buy tools to solve the problem. I try to learn new techniques. Nothing works. I can't ask for help. If I ask for help then people will see how little progress I've made and think so poorly of me that it'll be unrecoverable. I'm punching a wall. The wall doesn't budge. I break all the bones in my hand, but I keep punching. The wall doesn't budge. I continue to try to resolve the problem on my own. I can't. My sleep and diet have caught up to me. I can't even concentrate. I tell myself that if I just get a good night's sleep and eat some good food then tomorrow I can tackle the problem. Maybe that'll help me think more clearly such that I can finally solve the problem, however thinking this way only makes things worse.

***
I've now set conditions on how "comfortable" I need to feel to continue working. As the problem remains unresolved (or as new problems arise) I get superstisious about more and more conditions that must be met in order for me to have the optimal/just-right environment to actually, maybe, solve the problem this time. It doesn't work. Now my "routine" is several hours long. It takes all of my energy just to do the routine that I don't have any mental capacity to actually get any work done. I've convinced myself that this routine is necessary for work. Now I pretend to be working, but really I'm just compulsively doing my routine.

Now after doing the routine I see the immovable wall that is the problem I've been trying to solve. I get extreme anxiety when faced with this now. To resolve the anxiety I step away from the computer. If I just reduce the anxiety then maybe I can get back to working now that my routine is done. It provides momentary relief. I try to go back to working. Maybe I'm able to focus for a little while, but shortly the anxiety creeps up again, specifically whenever I run into any kind of resistance.

This snowballs into avoiding the computer altogether. Maybe I can still do my routine, but any inkling of actual progress is impossible. I'm so anxious about how long this as gone on, how bad my reputation is now, how incompetent I feel for being unable to solve it sooner.

I can't talk to people. I can't sit at the computer. I can't get any work done. I can't climb out of this hole. I've ended up doing exactly the same thing that I feared I would do. I'm no longer punching. Is this me now? Am I so fragile that I need 24/7 reassurance from others that I'm doing a good job. Do conditions always have to be "just right" in order for me to get any work done? I'll never accomplish anything like this. What happened to my resilliency? What happed to my work ethic? What happened to my future? How did I get so frail, so weak? I'll never accomplish anything like this. What's even the point? I'm worthless. I'm a waste of resources. The world would be better off without me. I only can't go through with any kind of suicide because I'm worried about the mess it would cause. I don't want to cause trauma for my family. I don't want to be a financial burden on them either. Afterall funerals are expensive. I guess I'll just fade away. If I remove my presence from the equation then I can just live long enough until people stop caring about me. Maybe then I can do whatever, or be whatever and it won't matter.
